Echo River Capital · water analyst

Data Centers & Water

Which technologies will actually cut the water that data centers use?

Echo River is a venture fund that invests in water technology. Over the summer I went through more than 20 technologies, from chip-level cooling down to where facilities get built, and compared them on maturity, cost, savings and whether existing sites could retrofit them.

I interviewed 11 engineers at Apple, Microsoft, 3M, Carollo Engineers and university labs to check what I was finding. That turned into a 24-page report with Clelia Poujade and Peter Yolles, a talk at WaterVent San Francisco, and an episode of 120Water's Make Water Work podcast.

Mundane Robotics · robotics intern

Driving a Robot With Your Feet

Flight pedals for the base, a SpaceMouse for the arm.

I built a teleoperation setup for a mobile manipulator with nine degrees of freedom. Flight pedals drive the base, and a 3D SpaceMouse moves the six-axis arm, so one person can run both at once.

I also compared the Genesis physics simulator with NVIDIA Isaac Sim on realism, stability and compute cost.

Recovered Potential · Stanford Climate Ventures

Who Pays for Recovered Nitrogen?

Customer discovery for a startup turning wastewater nitrogen into a product.

We did interviews, policy research and cost modeling to find where recovering nitrogen and phosphorus from wastewater pays off first. I worked with sustainability leads at JBS and Darling Ingredients, which led to the team's first two letters of intent.

Spirae · renewable energy data analyst

Forecasting Solar

A forecasting app for microgrid operators, from the database to the dashboard.

I built the whole thing in Python and PostgreSQL: pulling live site and weather data, running the forecasts, and showing operators production against forecast, with automated reports. I compared LSTM and Prophet models at two utility-scale sites, and the best got to 15.5 kW mean absolute error. A refactor later cut the code by 56%.

I wrote every line of it myself. No AI.
