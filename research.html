DELTA Group · Prof. Carlos Díaz-Marín

Water From Air

How much would it cost to get drinking water from a solar-powered harvester, and where would it be worth doing?

Cost at each of the 14,711 sites, for a device you could actually build: fixed tilt, with each day's schedule picked from the previous day's weather.

The device is a hydrogel loaded with lithium chloride. It soaks up water vapor overnight, and in the morning the sun heats it and drives the water out onto a condenser. I couple a heat and mass transfer model of that cycle to a levelized cost of water, then optimize the gel thickness, vapor gap, salt loading and daily schedule for every site.

I also built a heat and mass transfer model of the solar-thermal condenser following ISO 15099 and checked it against field measurements.

Running the full physics everywhere would take far too long, so I compress each site's weather with PCA, train a neural-network surrogate on 15,472 simulated years, and run Bayesian optimization on A100 GPUs on Stanford's Sherlock cluster.

Lithium From Brine

What does a tonne of battery-grade lithium carbonate cost to make, and what drives that cost?

I model a conventional brine operation from the wellfield to the dried product in Pyomo, using WaterTAP, IDAES and PrOMMiS. Reaktoro tracks the brine chemistry as it concentrates, and historical weather sets how big the evaporation ponds need to be. The whole plant is solved as one nonlinear cost minimization.

To check it, I ran the model for an operating plant on the Salar de Atacama, and it lines up closely with the producer's reported cost. Now I'm running it on brines from other salars to see how chemistry changes the cost. Paper in progress.

Choosing the Salt

Which salt, or mix of salts, should go in the hydrogel? It depends on the climate. This paper won the class Best Paper Award.

The harvester's gel can be loaded with LiCl, NaCl, CaCl₂, MgCl₂ or a blend. Given a site's night humidity, temperature and sunlight, we pick the blend and salt-to-polymer ratio that minimize the cost of water. The ZSR mixing rule handles the blended brine, a fixed-point loop solves for gel temperature, and SLSQP does the optimization. We used the epsilon-constraint method to trace the tradeoff between yield and cost.

I built the physical model, wrote the constraints and collected the climate and materials data. Veronica led the Pareto analysis and the writing.

Predicting Pitzer Coefficients

Can basic ion properties predict how a salt behaves in water, well enough to screen sorbents?

Pitzer coefficients describe how a dissolved salt deviates from ideal behavior, but they're only measured for a limited set of salts. We took 108 electrolytes with literature values, matched each to 15 ion properties (radii, molecular weight, charge type), and trained regression models to predict β⁽⁰⁾ and β⁽¹⁾.

Gradient boosting did best. Electrolyte type mattered most, then van der Waals radius. This feeds into my DELTA work on which salt properties control water uptake.
